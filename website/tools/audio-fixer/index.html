Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Windows 11 say "Audio services not responding"? +
This message appears when the Windows Audio service (Audiosrv) or the Windows Audio Endpoint Builder (AudioEndpointBuilder) stops responding to RPC queries. It is frequently triggered by buggy third-party audio drivers (Realtek, Nahimic, Dolby Atmos) or sleep/wake power glitches. Pexoris AudioFixer sets both services to Automatic and restarts them cleanly in the correct dependency sequence.
How does Pexoris AudioFixer solve "No audio output device is installed"? +
When Windows displays a red X on the speaker icon, it usually means AudioEndpointBuilder crashed or lost its device handle mapping in the registry. Clicking "⚡ 1-Click Audio Graph Reset" restarts the endpoint enumerator and triggers an automated Plug and Play (PnP) hardware rescan to re-initialize all DACs and audio ports.
What causes high CPU or memory usage in audiodg.exe? +
audiodg.exe is the Windows Audio Device Graph Isolation process responsible for hosting audio processing objects (APOs) such as equalization, spatial sound, and noise cancellation. Poorly written APOs frequently enter infinite loops or leak hundreds of megabytes of RAM. AudioFixer terminates hung audiodg.exe instances so Windows can spawn a clean, lightweight instance.
Will Pexoris AudioFixer work with USB sound cards and Bluetooth headsets? +
Yes. Pexoris AudioFixer operates at the core Windows Multimedia Device (MMDevice) layer. It restores sound across internal motherboard sound cards (Realtek, Conexant), USB DACs, gaming headsets, HDMI monitor speakers, and Bluetooth A2DP audio profiles.
Does Pexoris AudioFixer run in the background? +
No. Pexoris strictly adheres to a zero-resident standard. The executable performs the requested audio stack repairs directly on your operating system and terminates completely with 0% memory or CPU overhead.
